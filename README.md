## Overview

This repository contains an independent premedical educational portfolio of synthetic critical care scenarios developed for self-directed study in physiology-based reasoning and structured scenario analysis. Five scenario sets examine hemodynamic physiology, respiratory failure, multi-organ dysfunction, mechanical circulatory support concepts, and ethical dimensions of critical care from distinct educational perspectives.

Each scenario set is constructed entirely from published literature, textbook physiology, and educational synthesis — not from real patient encounters, institutional workflows, or clinical experience. None of the documents constitute clinical protocols, institutional guidelines, order sets, or decision-support tools. The author is a pre-medical applicant and not a licensed physician or other healthcare professional.

## Educational Architecture

This portfolio represents a premedical student's progressive study of multi-system physiology and structured educational analysis of physiological failure and escalation concepts. The five scenario sets examine distinct but related dimensions of critical care physiology:

- **Case Sets 01–02** address foundational and advanced hemodynamic reasoning — shock classification, differential analysis under physiologic uncertainty, airway physiology, and the literature-based rationale for escalation in multi-system failure.

- **Case Sets 03–04** address postoperative cardiothoracic physiology — ventricular function after surgical intervention, mechanical device physiology, graft injury, and the interaction between surgical complexity and organ support as described in the educational literature.

- **Case Set 05** addresses mechanical circulatory support concepts and goals-of-care reasoning — examining the intersection of advanced physiologic support with ethical reflection, comfort-focused care, and the humanistic dimensions of critical illness.

Each scenario set has an independent educational contribution. Together they form a coherent scholarly portfolio demonstrating progressive engagement with physiology-based reasoning, multi-system integration, and ethical awareness — rather than a collection of independent exercises. This work reflects a premedical student's self-directed study and is not intended as clinical training or preparation for unsupervised practice.

Management reasoning throughout the portfolio is framed as educational discussion of what interventions might be considered in supervised settings, not as clinical directives. Language reflects awareness that all clinical decisions belong to licensed practitioners.

## Interpretive Caution

The scenarios in this repository are synthetic educational constructions built from textbook physiology, published literature, and educational synthesis. They are not derived from real clinical encounters, real patient data, or institutional workflows, and they should not be used as clinical references.

Management discussion throughout the portfolio reflects literature-informed educational reasoning rather than validated clinical protocols or institutional practice. Simplification, abstraction, and pedagogic emphasis are present throughout because this is a premedical educational portfolio, not a clinical reference or professional training document. No formal evidence-grading methodology was applied.

No part of this repository should be interpreted as prescriptive clinical guidance, performance targets, or standards of care of any kind.

## Evidence Base

Scenarios and learning frameworks draw on published educational resources including critical care textbooks (Marino's *The ICU Book*, UpToDate), society guidelines (SCCM, CHEST, Surviving Sepsis Campaign, ARDSNet, Brain Trauma Foundation), landmark randomized trials cited in the critical care literature, and review articles on shock physiology, mechanical ventilation, mechanical circulatory support, and neurocritical care.

These sources inform the educational content of the scenarios. The repository is a narrative educational synthesis, not a systematic review or formal evidence appraisal.

Full references are provided in `references/bibliography.md`.
