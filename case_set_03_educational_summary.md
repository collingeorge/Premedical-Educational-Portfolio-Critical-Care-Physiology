# Case Set 03: Educational Summary and Synthesis

---

## Scenario 1 — POD0 CABG ×3, Vasoplegia + Hypothermia + Bleeding Risk

**Scenario progression:**
- Rewarmed from 35.8 → 36.4°C (still slightly below goal)
- MAP now 64–68 on NE 0.08 + vaso 0.03
- Lactate improved slightly: 3.4 → 3.0
- Chest tube output decreased from 140 → 110 mL/hr
- TTE: LV moderately depressed (EF ~40–45%), RV normal; no tamponade

**Key management questions for discussion:**
- Continuing rewarming to ≥36.5°C would be appropriate
- Reassessing pressor needs as SVR improves would be a reasonable next step
- Chest tube output remains borderline; continued hourly monitoring would be indicated
- Repeating coag panel + fibrinogen would be appropriate for ongoing evaluation
- Reassessing for possible extubation could be considered if lactate continues downward and pressors can be reduced

**Discussion priorities:**
1. Pressor weaning as temperature normalizes would be expected
2. Lactate trending to goal <2.5 would guide management
3. Readiness for SBT/possible extubation would be evaluated

## Scenario 2 — VA-ECMO for Fulminant Myocarditis, Rising Lactate + LV Distension Risk

**Scenario progression:**
- Lactate plateaued but not improving: 5.3 → 5.1
- SvO₂ improved from 58% → 63% after flow increased to 4.3 L/min
- UOP remained low; CRRT initiated
- LDH remains elevated; plasma-free Hgb pending
- TTE: LV severely dilated, AV not opening; distension persists

**Key management questions for discussion:**
- Persistent LV distension would necessitate escalation of LV unloading strategy
- IABP vs Impella selection would require multidisciplinary evaluation
- Oxygenator performance is borderline — oxygenator change-out could be considered if gradient widens
- CRRT running even balance

**Discussion priorities:**
1. Evaluation for mechanical LV unloading (IABP/Impella) would be a key question
2. LDH and PFH trending to determine threshold for oxygenator replacement
3. Flow maintenance ≥4.3 L/min, adjusted based on SvO₂ and lactate

## Scenario 3 — POD2 HM3 LVAD With New RV Failure

**Scenario progression:**
- Dobutamine 2 µg/kg/min started; CVP improved 15 → 12
- PI stable at ~2.0; no suction events
- Net fluid –300 mL over the scenario period
- MAP steady 70–72 on NE 0.03
- Renal function stable (Cr unchanged)

**Key management questions for discussion:**
- Reassessing RV function on limited echo would be appropriate
- Whether to continue dobutamine or transition to milrinone is a relevant clinical question
- Extubation readiness assessment would be appropriate after RV reassessment

**Discussion priorities:**
1. RV preload reduction if CVP remains >10 would be a management goal
2. Gentle diuresis continuation (–500 mL target) would be appropriate
3. Extubation could be considered if PI improves >2.5

## Scenario 4 — Severe ARDS, Re-proned, Hypercapnia Post-Supination

**Scenario progression:**
- Re-proned; oxygenation improved (PF ~90 → 115)
- pH stabilized at 7.24 with permissive hypercapnia
- Switching to pressure control reduced DP to 16–17 but cannot reach <15 yet
- No hemodynamic instability on NE 0.06

**Key management questions for discussion:**
- Continuing the prone session through mid-day would be appropriate
- Re-evaluating for ECMO would be warranted if pH <7.2 or oxygenation worsens when supinated
- CT Chest could be useful if a sudden compliance drop is observed

**Discussion priorities:**
1. Strict lung-protective ventilation would be maintained
2. ECMO evaluation would be considered if DP remains >18
3. Paralysis continuation until at least the 24h mark would be expected

## Scenario 5 — POD1 Hemiarch Repair, Hypertension + Possible Renal Malperfusion

**Scenario progression:**
- Two 250 mL boluses increased CVP to 6; UOP improved modestly (15–20 → 30 mL/hr)
- SBP now 110–125 on nicardipine 3–4 mg/hr + esmolol
- Back pain resolved after BP control
- Creatinine stable; no red flags

**Key management questions for discussion:**
- Whether CTA is still needed would depend on UOP trajectory; if UOP increases to >35–40, deferral could be reasonable
- Careful titration of antihypertensives to maintain SBP 100–120 would be important
- Continued frequent UOP and creatinine trending would be appropriate

**Discussion priorities:**
1. Tight BP control would remain a priority
2. Repeat BMP + lactate would guide ongoing evaluation
3. CTA would be indicated only if UOP worsens or back pain recurs

## Scenario 6 — POD0–1 Bilateral Lung Transplant, PGD Grade 2–3, Low Compliance

**Scenario progression:**
- PEEP successfully reduced to 6 with stable oxygenation
- DP improved from 22 → 17
- Net fluid –450 mL with diuretics
- Tacrolimus level pending; hemodynamics stable

**Key management questions for discussion:**
- Continuing a net-negative fluid strategy would be appropriate
- Bronchoscopy + BAL would be indicated for graft evaluation
- ECMO candidacy evaluation would be warranted if gas exchange worsens

**Discussion priorities:**
1. Net negative –1 to –1.5 L would be a reasonable volume goal
2. DP <15 maintenance would be prioritized; VT reduction if needed
3. Tacrolimus level monitoring and infusion adjustment would be ongoing

## Scenario 7 — Septic Shock, Open Abdomen, Refractory Shock With Venous Congestion

**Scenario progression:**
- Vasopressin started; MAP improved from 54 → 63
- Lactate improved 6.5 → 5.9 but still high
- CRRT running; net even due to hemodynamic fragility
- Bladder pressure 10–12; no significant ACS signal
- No new abdominal distension or worsening tenderness

**Key management questions for discussion:**
- Reassessing source control — whether repeat imaging or operative intervention is needed — would be an important discussion point
- Adding epinephrine could be considered if MAP declines again
- Evaluating the infectious workup and considering antifungal escalation would be appropriate

**Discussion priorities:**
1. Lactate + SvO₂ trending to assess trajectory
2. Clinical abdominal reassessment at least q4h
3. CTA abdomen/pelvis would be warranted if lactate rises again or MAP deteriorates

## Scenario 8 — Aneurysmal SAH (PBD2/POD1) + Takotsubo Cardiomyopathy

**Scenario progression:**
- MAP maintained 70–75 with NE 0.05
- CPP stable 65–70; ICP 14 unchanged
- No neuro exam changes
- Sodium stable at 150 on 3% at 30 mL/hr
- No arrhythmias; lactate normal

**Key management questions for discussion:**
- Transcranial Dopplers for vasospasm screening would be appropriate
- Anticipating MAP dip with nimodipine doses and preparing for pressor uptitration would be prudent
- Extubation timing would likely be deferred to allow further hemodynamic stabilization

**Discussion priorities:**
1. CPP ≥65 maintenance would be a key ongoing goal
2. Frequent neurologic reassessment would be important
3. Diuresis would be avoided; euvolemia maintenance would be prioritized

## Cross-Scenario Educational Themes

### Scenarios requiring focused discussion
- **Scenario 2:** LV distension + ECMO membrane dysfunction — may require unloading device + oxygenator change
- **Scenario 4:** Near ECMO threshold
- **Scenario 7:** Persistent shock; source control and CRRT critical
- **Scenario 8:** Vasospasm risk peaking from this point onward

### Diagnostic and procedural learning points
- **Scenario 6:** Bronchoscopy + BAL
- **Scenario 8:** TCDs
- **Scenario 1:** Repeat TTE if pressor needs escalate
- **Scenario 5:** CTA only if UOP worsens
- **Scenario 2:** Possibly oxygenator changeout and device consult
- **Scenario 3:** Limited echo for RV reassessment

### Device Checks
- **ECMO (Scenario 2):** Hourly flows + gradient trending
- **LVAD (Scenario 3):** PI and suction watch
- **CRRT (Scenarios 2 & 7):** Filter pressures + UF settings
- **Ventilation:** Scenarios 4 and 6 require Pplat/DP trending every 2–4 hours

## Educational Synthesis

Scenarios 2, 4, 7, and 8 present the most complex ongoing management challenges and serve as the richest cases for discussion of physiologic reasoning. All eight scenarios have clearly defined management considerations and discussion priorities that illustrate key principles in cardiothoracic critical care decision-making.
