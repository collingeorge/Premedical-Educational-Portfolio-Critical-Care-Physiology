# Simulated case set: Advanced hemodynamic reasoning

Entirely synthetic independent premedical study material. Revised 27 October 2026. The author confirmed that scenarios are independent of real encounters/charts. This reconstruction preserves the earlier topic coverage while withdrawing unsupported medication doses, device adjustments and automatic escalation thresholds. Earlier versions remain in Git history.

## How to use these exercises

Identify mechanisms, missing information and the limits of each inference. These are discussion prompts rather than management plans. A completed worksheet demonstrates study, not competence to diagnose or treat patients. Use the [source guide](references/bibliography.md) and [self-assessment rubric](learning_objectives/self_assessment_rubric.md).

## Scenario 1: Shock immediately after CABG

[Paired synthetic application examples](APPLIED_CASES.md#icu-02-01). [Integrated workup and test reasoning](ICU_CTICU_WORKUP_CASES.md#icu-02-01).

**Synthetic vignette:** A fictional adult after coronary bypass has hypotension, low estimated output, hypothermia and ongoing drain output.

**Reasoning questions**

1. Compare tone, pump, filling and obstructive mechanisms.
2. What makes filling pressures and echocardiography context dependent?
3. How could bleeding, temperature and measurement conditions alter the interpretation?

**Interpretation boundary:** A single cardiac-index or drain-output cutoff is not a complete treatment rule.

## Scenario 2: VA-ECMO after myocardial infarction

[Paired synthetic application examples](APPLIED_CASES.md#icu-02-02). [Integrated workup and test reasoning](ICU_CTICU_WORKUP_CASES.md#icu-02-02).

**Synthetic vignette:** A fictional adult on VA-ECMO has improving lactate but oliguria and concern about limb perfusion.

**Reasoning questions**

1. Separate systemic flow from regional perfusion.
2. Which sampling locations matter when investigating differential oxygenation?
3. How might LV loading, congestion and cannula-related problems overlap?

**Interpretation boundary:** Post-oxygenator oxygenation does not alone establish upper-body oxygenation. Flow changes, cannula procedures and renal-support prescriptions require an ECMO team.

## Scenario 3: Early LVAD and RV function

[Paired synthetic application examples](APPLIED_CASES.md#icu-02-03). [Integrated workup and test reasoning](ICU_CTICU_WORKUP_CASES.md#icu-02-03).

**Synthetic vignette:** A fictional adult after LVAD implantation has changing displayed flow and pulsatility with borderline RV function.

**Reasoning questions**

1. What do displayed device values measure or estimate?
2. How could RV function, preload, afterload, rhythm and device settings affect the observations?
3. What imaging and device-team information is missing?

**Interpretation boundary:** No universal pulsatility-index target, CVP range, speed change or anticoagulation sequence is established here.

## Scenario 4: Pneumonia-associated ARDS

[Paired synthetic application examples](APPLIED_CASES.md#icu-02-04). [Integrated workup and test reasoning](ICU_CTICU_WORKUP_CASES.md#icu-02-04).

**Synthetic vignette:** A fictional adult with ARDS has a reported P:F ratio near 87, increased driving pressure and vasopressor support.

**Reasoning questions**

1. Check how the ratio and pressure measurements were obtained.
2. Contrast recruitment and overdistension effects on gas exchange and RV loading.
3. What evidence and patient factors govern specialist discussion of prone positioning, paralysis or ECMO?

**Interpretation boundary:** A reported ratio is not a self-contained rescue protocol. Ventilator mode names do not guarantee lung protection.

## Scenario 5: Aortic repair, hypertension and AKI

[Paired synthetic application examples](APPLIED_CASES.md#icu-02-05). [Integrated workup and test reasoning](ICU_CTICU_WORKUP_CASES.md#icu-02-05).

**Synthetic vignette:** A fictional adult after type A dissection repair develops hypertension, oliguria and rising creatinine.

**Reasoning questions**

1. Consider renal malperfusion, congestion, hypoperfusion, obstruction and medication effects.
2. Why can a low CVP not establish prerenal AKI or justify repeated fluid boluses?
3. How do graft protection and organ perfusion create competing questions?

**Interpretation boundary:** No blood-pressure prescription or delay-to-imaging rule is supplied.

## Scenario 6: Lung transplantation and graft dysfunction

[Paired synthetic application examples](APPLIED_CASES.md#icu-02-06). [Integrated workup and test reasoning](ICU_CTICU_WORKUP_CASES.md#icu-02-06).

**Synthetic vignette:** A fictional lung-transplant recipient develops poor compliance, impaired gas exchange and fluid accumulation.

**Reasoning questions**

1. What timing, imaging and exclusion criteria are needed before grading primary graft dysfunction?
2. How would recipient/donor size and ventilator mechanics affect interpretation?
3. How could recruitment, overdistension, congestion and RV function interact?

**Interpretation boundary:** Blanket PEEP reduction, fixed diuresis targets and an assumed immunosuppression regimen are withdrawn. Transplant-specific care is specialist work.

## Scenario 7: Sepsis and an open abdomen

[Paired synthetic application examples](APPLIED_CASES.md#icu-02-07). [Integrated workup and test reasoning](ICU_CTICU_WORKUP_CASES.md#icu-02-07).

**Synthetic vignette:** A fictional adult with abdominal sepsis has worsening shock, oliguria and positive fluid balance.

**Reasoning questions**

1. What source-control information is missing?
2. How does intra-abdominal pressure relate to new organ dysfunction?
3. How would fluid responsiveness, fluid tolerance and venous congestion be distinguished?

**Interpretation boundary:** A pressure number alone does not diagnose abdominal compartment syndrome or authorize altering a surgical dressing.

## Scenario 8: SAH with stress cardiomyopathy

[Paired synthetic application examples](APPLIED_CASES.md#icu-02-08). [Integrated workup and test reasoning](ICU_CTICU_WORKUP_CASES.md#icu-02-08).

**Synthetic vignette:** A fictional adult after aneurysm treatment has cardiac dysfunction and concern about cerebral perfusion.

**Reasoning questions**

1. Verify the relationship among MAP, ICP and the reported CPP.
2. Distinguish prevention from treatment of symptomatic delayed cerebral ischemia.
3. How might cardiac dysfunction constrain hemodynamic interventions?

**Interpretation boundary:** No prophylactic hypertension, hypervolemia or fixed hypernatremia regimen is supplied.
