# Simulated Case Set 03: Postoperative Cardiothoracic Scenarios
## 8-Scenario Comprehensive Analysis

Educational scenario set for physiology-based reasoning

---

## Scenario 1 — POD0 CABG ×3, Vasoplegic-Predominant Shock + Hypothermia + Moderate Bleeding

**Status:**
- MAP 58–62 on NE 0.10 + vaso 0.03
- SVR 640, CI 2.1
- Temp 35.8°C
- Chest tube: 140 mL/hr
- Lactate 2.9 → 3.4 rising
- CVP 8

**Assessment:** Vasoplegia likely predominant but LV contribution not excluded; hypothermia worsening vasodilation and coagulopathy. Moderate ongoing bleeding.

**Reasoning and management considerations:**
- Immediate bedside TTE would be indicated (LV function, RV function, preload, septal position)
- If EF >40%, continuing dual vasopressors would be appropriate; if CI <2.2, adding epinephrine at 0.02–0.03 µg/kg/min would be a reasonable consideration
- Rewarming to >36.5°C (forced air + warmed fluids) would be expected to improve SVR
- Preload: a small bolus of 250 mL would be reasonable only if echo shows underfilling
- Hourly chest tube monitoring would be appropriate; transfusion to maintain Hgb >8, fibrinogen >150 is generally indicated
- Extubation would be deferred until the patient is warm, on minimal pressors, and lactate is improving

**Educational discussion goals:** MAP ≥65, CI >2.2, lactate trending down, temp >36.5°C, chest tube <100 mL/hr, transition toward single pressor.

## Scenario 2 — VA-ECMO (Fulminant Viral Myocarditis), Rising Lactate + Concern for LV Distension + Early Circuit Failure

**Status:**
- ECMO flow 4.0 L/min
- Lactate 4.1 → 5.3 rising
- SvO₂ 58%
- UOP 20 mL/hr
- LDH 610 + widening pre/post oxygenator gradient

**Assessment:** Global DO₂ inadequate despite ECMO; high suspicion for LV distension, hemolysis, or membrane dysfunction.

**Reasoning and management considerations:**
- Urgent bedside TTE to assess LV distension, AV opening, RV function would be indicated
- A trial increase of flows to 4.3–4.5 L/min could be considered if tolerated
- Optimizing LV unloading through afterload reduction (clevidipine/nitroprusside) would be appropriate; early consideration of IABP or Impella would be warranted if persistent distension is identified
- Circuit evaluation including plasma-free Hgb, haptoglobin, and perfusion assessment for membrane thrombus would be indicated — oxygenator change-out would be appropriate if the gradient widens
- CRRT initiation would be a reasonable consideration for metabolic control and renal venous congestion
- Maintaining MAP 65–70 on NE ± vasopressin would be a standard approach

**Educational discussion goals:** Lactate ↓10% in 4 hrs, SvO₂ ≥65%, no LV distension, stable ECMO gradients, UOP improving or CRRT running.

## Scenario 3 — POD2 HeartMate 3 LVAD, New RV Dysfunction — Rising CVP, Falling PI

**Status:**
- Speed 5200 RPM, Flow 4.6 L/min
- PI 2.5 → 1.8
- CVP 15
- MAP 70 on NE 0.03
- UOP borderline; creatinine rising

**Assessment:** RV failure is limiting LVAD preload; LVAD is effectively over-pumping relative to RV capacity. Renal venous congestion emerging.

**Reasoning and management considerations:**
- Maintaining current speed would be appropriate; if suction events occur, reducing by 100–200 RPM would be a reasonable adjustment
- Gentle diuresis with furosemide 40 mg IV, targeting net –500 mL, would be indicated; aggressive fluid shifts should be avoided
- Dobutamine initiation at 2–3 µg/kg/min (or milrinone if BP tolerates) would be a reasonable consideration for RV support
- Inhaled pulmonary vasodilator therapy could be considered if RV pressures remain elevated
- Extubation would be deferred pending improved RV function and PI stabilization

**Educational discussion goals:** CVP <12, PI >2.0–2.5, MAP 70–75, adequate UOP, no suction events, improved RV geometry on repeat limited echo.

## Scenario 4 — Severe ARDS, Hypercapnia + Oxygenation Failure Post-Proning — Near ECMO Thresholds

**Status:**
- PaCO₂ rising despite RR 28, VT 6 mL/kg
- PF ~85–95 after supination
- DP 18–22, Pplat 30
- On paralysis + deep sedation

**Assessment:** Marked dead-space ventilation and low recruitable lung volume. Supination causing CO₂ retention. Approaching VV ECMO criteria.

**Reasoning and management considerations:**
- Re-proning for another 16-hour session would be strongly indicated
- Switching to pressure control to cap pressures would be appropriate; targeting DP <15, Pplat <30 is consistent with lung-protective strategy
- VT 4–6 mL/kg would be appropriate, maintaining RR 28 while avoiding auto-PEEP
- Permissive hypercapnia would be acceptable if pH ≥7.20; if pH <7.20, ECMO consultation would be warranted
- A conservative fluid strategy targeting –500 to –1,000 mL would be reasonable if tolerated

**Educational discussion goals:** Stable pH ≥7.20, DP <15, FiO₂ <80%, no hemodynamic instability. Early ECMO activation if thresholds met.

## Scenario 5 — POD1 Ascending + Hemiarch Repair, Severe Hypertension + Oliguria + Concern for Renal Malperfusion

**Status:**
- SBP 150–165
- HR 78 on esmolol
- CVP 5
- UOP 15–20 mL/hr
- Creatinine rising
- New back pain

**Assessment:** Dangerous postoperative hypertension; likely underfilled. Back pain + oliguria raise concern for renal malperfusion or extension of dissection.

**Reasoning and management considerations:**
- Administering 250 mL crystalloid and reassessing would be a reasonable initial step; repeating if CVP <6 would be appropriate
- Continuing esmolol for HR and initiating nicardipine or clevidipine for SBP 100–120 (MAP ≥65) would be standard management
- A small NE uptitration could be considered if MAP drops
- If UOP fails to improve once CVP reaches 6–8, a STAT CTA abdomen/pelvis to rule out renal artery malperfusion would be warranted
- Diuretics should be avoided until malperfusion is excluded

**Educational discussion goals:** SBP 100–120, HR <80, MAP ≥65, CVP 6–8, UOP >35 mL/hr or CTA performed. No bleeding escalation.

## Scenario 6 — POD0–1 Bilateral Lung Transplant, PGD Grade 2–3 + Low Compliance + Volume Overload

**Status:**
- PF 110–120
- PEEP 8 → DP 22
- Net +2.5 L
- On minimal NE

**Assessment:** Early PGD from ischemia–reperfusion injury. Transplanted lungs highly vulnerable to VILI. Volume overload is worsening graft edema.

**Reasoning and management considerations:**
- Lowering PEEP to 6 → 5 if tolerated would be a reasonable approach
- VT 4–6 mL/kg would be appropriate; reducing to 4 if DP >15 is consistent with protective ventilation
- Maintaining Pplat <28 is important; recruitment maneuvers should generally be avoided in this setting
- Furosemide initiation at 20–40 mg would be indicated, with escalation to a drip if insufficient; a goal of net –500 to –1,000 mL would be reasonable
- Maintaining MAP ≥65 with NE (rather than fluids) would be the preferred approach
- Continuation of tacrolimus, basiliximab, and steroids would be standard; bronchoscopy/BAL would be appropriate for evaluation when clinically feasible
- Early ECMO activation would be warranted if PF <100 on FiO₂ 1.0 or pH <7.2

**Educational discussion goals:** FiO₂ <50%, DP <15, net –0.5 to –1 L, CVP 6–8, stable hemodynamics.

## Scenario 7 — Septic Shock + Ischemic Bowel + Open Abdomen, Refractory Hypotension + Rising Lactate + High Venous Congestion

**Status:**
- MAP 54 on NE 0.18
- Lactate 6.5 rising
- CVP 14
- UOP 5–10 mL/hr

**Assessment:** Severe distributive shock with venous congestion; high concern for ongoing ischemia, inadequate source control, or functional abdominal compartment physiology.

**Reasoning and management considerations:**
- Adding vasopressin 0.03 would be a standard next step in this setting
- If MAP <65, adding epinephrine at 0.03–0.1 µg/kg/min would be a reasonable escalation
- Hydrocortisone 50 mg q6h initiation would be appropriate in refractory vasodilatory shock
- CRRT initiation would be indicated for metabolic control and to reduce venous congestion
- Further fluid administration should be avoided; bladder pressure monitoring would be appropriate; if >12 or physiology worsening, treatment as functional ACS would be warranted
- Continuation of broad-spectrum antibiotics would be appropriate; adding micafungin could be considered if clinical deterioration occurs
- Early re-exploration would be warranted if source control remains uncertain

**Educational discussion goals:** MAP ≥65 on fewer agents, lactate trending down, CRRT functioning, no ACS, no new ischemia.

## Scenario 8 — PBD2/POD1 Aneurysmal SAH + Takotsubo Cardiomyopathy, CPP–Afterload Conflict + High Vasospasm Risk

**Status:**
- ICP 14
- CPP 66
- MAP 70 on NE 0.03–0.05
- EF ~30% (Takotsubo)
- Na 150
- PSV 10/5

**Assessment:** High-risk vasospasm window. CPP goal ≥65 must be maintained without precipitating cardiac collapse. Nimodipine drops MAP; Takotsubo limits allowable afterload.

**Reasoning and management considerations:**
- Targeting MAP 70–75 and CPP ≥65 would be appropriate
- Continuing NE 0.03–0.05 would be standard; adding vasopressin if NE >0.08 is required would be a reasonable approach
- Anticipating MAP drop from nimodipine and considering preemptive NE uptitration would be prudent
- Sodium goal of 145–155 with continuation of 3% at 30 mL/hr would be appropriate
- Hourly neuro exams would be indicated; CTA would be warranted immediately for any exam change
- Extubation would be deferred due to hemodynamic and neurocardiac fragility
- Strict euvolemia at 80–100 mL/hr would be appropriate; diuresis should be avoided

**Educational discussion goals:** CPP 65–70, MAP 70–75, ICP <20, Na 145–155, stable neuro exam, no cardiogenic deterioration.

## Cross-Scenario Educational Themes

**Hemodynamics:** High pressor burden in Scenarios 1, 2, 7. LVAD (Scenario 3) and ECMO (Scenario 2) require hourly hemodynamic data + device checks.

**Ventilation:** ARDS (Scenario 4) and PGD (Scenario 6) are the highest risk for refractory gas exchange failure. ECMO readiness for Scenarios 4 and 6.

**Renal:** CRRT active or needed for Scenarios 2 and 7; possible malperfusion in Scenario 5.

**Neuro:** Scenario 8 requires strict hourly neuro monitoring and anticipatory pressor management for CPP.

**Surgical / Source Control:** Scenario 7 may need re-exploration. Scenario 5 may need urgent CTA for malperfusion.

---

## Key Educational Discussion Points

**Device/Perfusion:** ECMO flows falling, LV distension (Scenario 2), suction events (Scenario 3).

**Ventilation:** pH <7.20 (Scenario 4 or 6) despite optimization would be an indication for ECMO evaluation.

**Renal:** Oliguria after preload optimization would warrant CTA or CRRT depending on the clinical scenario.

**Neuro:** CPP <60, new anisocoria, any exam change (Scenario 8).

**Hemodynamics:** MAP <60 on dual agents (any scenario). Lactate rising on >2 consecutive draws (any scenario).

Considerations warranting urgent reassessment by the supervising clinical team: any acute hemodynamic decompensation, new device malfunction, acute neurological change, or refractory gas exchange failure despite maximal optimization.
