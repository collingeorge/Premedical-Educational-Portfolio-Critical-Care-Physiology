# Simulated case set: Multisystem critical care

Entirely synthetic independent premedical study material. Revised 27 October 2026. The author confirmed that scenarios are independent of real encounters/charts. This reconstruction preserves the earlier topic coverage while withdrawing unsupported medication doses, device adjustments and automatic escalation thresholds. Earlier versions remain in Git history.

## How to use these exercises

Identify mechanisms, missing information and the limits of each inference. These are discussion prompts rather than management plans. A completed worksheet demonstrates study, not competence to diagnose or treat patients. Use the [source guide](references/bibliography.md) and [self-assessment rubric](learning_objectives/self_assessment_rubric.md).

## Scenario 1: Postoperative hemodynamic instability

[Paired synthetic application examples](APPLIED_CASES.md#icu-01-01). [Integrated workup and test reasoning](ICU_CTICU_WORKUP_CASES.md#icu-01-01).

**Synthetic vignette:** A fictional postoperative adult develops hypotension, rising lactate and chest-drain output. Cardiac function and the bleeding source have not been established.

**Reasoning questions**

1. Compare hemorrhagic, cardiogenic, obstructive and distributive mechanisms. Which may coexist?
2. What can bedside imaging add, and what are its limitations after cardiac surgery?
3. Which observations distinguish an isolated pressure change from impaired perfusion?

**Interpretation boundary:** Tamponade is an obstructive mechanism. A drain-output number alone does not establish a re-exploration threshold; postoperative tamponade should not be reduced to an automatic pericardiocentesis instruction.

## Scenario 2: DNI status and respiratory distress

[Paired synthetic application examples](APPLIED_CASES.md#icu-01-02). [Integrated workup and test reasoning](ICU_CTICU_WORKUP_CASES.md#icu-01-02).

**Synthetic vignette:** An older fictional adult with COPD and pneumonia is distressed and has difficulty tolerating non-invasive ventilation. DNR/DNI is documented; other treatment preferences are unknown.

**Reasoning questions**

1. Clarify the directive, decision-making capacity and the patient’s goals for this episode.
2. Compare a time-limited treatment trial with separately chosen comfort-only care.
3. What might explain mask intolerance, and why might an oxygen interface not substitute for ventilatory assistance?

**Interpretation boundary:** DNR/DNI does not itself mean comfort-only care or justify accepting hypoxemia or ignoring respiratory depression. Palliative support may accompany active treatment.

## Scenario 3: Worsening septic shock

[Paired synthetic application examples](APPLIED_CASES.md#icu-01-03). [Integrated workup and test reasoning](ICU_CTICU_WORKUP_CASES.md#icu-01-03).

**Synthetic vignette:** A fictional adult with suspected infection has increasing vasopressor requirements, mottling, oliguria and rising lactate.

**Reasoning questions**

1. What information is needed about infection, source control, fluid exposure and alternative shock mechanisms?
2. Which dynamic responsiveness tests are valid in this setting, and what do they fail to establish about fluid tolerance?
3. How can lactate production, clearance and microcirculatory findings alter interpretation?

**Interpretation boundary:** A lactate trend is not specific for tissue hypoxia. The current sepsis guideline is not interchangeable with an unconditional three-drug escalation rule.

## Scenario 4: Rib fractures and impaired ventilation

[Paired synthetic application examples](APPLIED_CASES.md#icu-01-04). [Integrated workup and test reasoning](ICU_CTICU_WORKUP_CASES.md#icu-01-04).

**Synthetic vignette:** A fictional trauma patient has chest-wall pain, shallow breathing and increasing oxygen needs. Associated injuries are incompletely described.

**Reasoning questions**

1. Separate pain-limited ventilation from contusion, pneumothorax, hemothorax and other causes.
2. How would regional and systemic analgesia be compared with attention to bleeding risk and monitoring?
3. Which missing observations would change the urgency of expert reassessment?

**Interpretation boundary:** Pain relief, ventilation and oxygenation are distinct outcomes. This exercise supplies no regional-block or opioid regimen.

## Scenario 5: Myocarditis and shock

[Paired synthetic application examples](APPLIED_CASES.md#icu-01-05). [Integrated workup and test reasoning](ICU_CTICU_WORKUP_CASES.md#icu-01-05).

**Synthetic vignette:** A fictional adult with suspected fulminant myocarditis has poor perfusion despite initial support. Ventricular function, rhythm and the cause of inflammation need reassessment.

**Reasoning questions**

1. What reversible causes and competing shock mechanisms must be considered?
2. Contrast what VA and VV extracorporeal support are designed to support.
3. What information would a specialist team need before considering mechanical support?

**Interpretation boundary:** A poor ejection fraction or lactate value alone is not a device-eligibility algorithm.

## Scenario 6: Pancreatitis with respiratory and renal dysfunction

[Paired synthetic application examples](APPLIED_CASES.md#icu-01-06). [Integrated workup and test reasoning](ICU_CTICU_WORKUP_CASES.md#icu-01-06).

**Synthetic vignette:** A fictional postoperative adult with pancreatitis has pain, positive fluid balance, worsening breathing and rising creatinine.

**Reasoning questions**

1. How could intravascular depletion and tissue congestion coexist?
2. What does the WATERFALL trial address, and how does its population differ from this vignette?
3. What missing information is needed about enteral tolerance, electrolytes and intra-abdominal pressure?

**Interpretation boundary:** Routine aggressive fluids, a fixed infusion rate, automatic calcium replacement and a universal epidural preference are not established by this scenario.

## Scenario 7: Brain injury with aspiration and airway decline

[Paired synthetic application examples](APPLIED_CASES.md#icu-01-07). [Integrated workup and test reasoning](ICU_CTICU_WORKUP_CASES.md#icu-01-07).

**Synthetic vignette:** A fictional adult with traumatic brain injury develops reduced alertness and suspected aspiration.

**Reasoning questions**

1. How might hypoxemia, hypotension, carbon dioxide and sedation confound the neurologic assessment?
2. Why are injury-specific targets and rescue measures different from routine prophylactic hyperventilation?
3. Which observations require urgent specialist evaluation rather than a portfolio-based algorithm?

**Interpretation boundary:** Traumatic-brain-injury guidance is condition-specific. A CPP target cannot be transferred automatically to every neurologic disorder.

## Scenario 8: Sepsis with ARDS and kidney injury

[Paired synthetic application examples](APPLIED_CASES.md#icu-01-08). [Integrated workup and test reasoning](ICU_CTICU_WORKUP_CASES.md#icu-01-08).

**Synthetic vignette:** A fictional adult with pneumonia has bilateral opacities, impaired gas exchange, positive fluid balance and kidney dysfunction.

**Reasoning questions**

1. Which measurements are needed before classifying ARDS severity?
2. How do ventilation mechanics, dyssynchrony, sedation and hemodynamics interact?
3. How would urgent renal-support indications be distinguished from an automatic early-dialysis response to fluid balance?

**Interpretation boundary:** FiO2 alone does not give a P:F ratio. P:F below 150 is not identical to the Berlin severe category. Neuromuscular blockade and renal support are not automatic responses to a single number.
