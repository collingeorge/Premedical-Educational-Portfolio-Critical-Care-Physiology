# ICU/CTICU worked laboratory examples

**Evidence labels:** [Guideline/consensus recommendation](EVIDENCE_CLASSIFICATION.md#recommendations) (strength and population stated), **general clinical knowledge**, **context-dependent clinical practice**, and **synthetic example**. A case or original diagram is not a standardized treatment protocol.

28 October 2026. These 11 worked examples extend the [18 procedure pairs](CTICU_PROCEDURE_EXAMPLES.md) and [42 integrated ICU topics](ICU_CTICU_WORKUP_CASES.md). People, numerical results and trends are wholly synthetic; no clinical outcome is claimed. These are explanations of source-supported clinical reasoning, not quizzes or an order set.

Choose an investigation to answer a clinical question that may change care. Review prior results, specimen/site, timing, current support, exposures and local assay ranges. A result supports or challenges a diagnosis; it does not independently diagnose every cause or prescribe treatment. Urgent assessment and treatment can proceed in parallel with investigation. Frequency depends on instability and the decision at hand.

<a id="bleeding"></a>
## Postoperative bleeding and anemia

**Paired case context — synthetic example:** The CABG male example has increasing drain loss; the valve-surgery female example has pallor and a falling pressure.

**Investigation and purpose — context-dependent clinical practice:** CBC (hemoglobin/hematocrit and platelets); fibrinogen and appropriate coagulation studies if bleeding is clinically suspected. Compatibility testing is considered when transfusion may be needed.

**Illustrative finding — synthetic data / clinical interpretation:** A fictional hemoglobin decrease from 10 to 8 g/dL, interpreted with timing, dilution, drain losses and perfusion, raises an anemia/blood-loss question. It is not a transfusion threshold.

**Effect on care — context-dependent clinical practice:** The team evaluates a surgical source and clinically relevant coagulopathy; selected viscoelastic testing can help guide a local hemostatic pathway. A laboratory result cannot exclude tamponade or replace surgical review.

**Classification note:** General clinical knowledge and context-dependent bleeding assessment; the fictional hemoglobin change is not a standardized transfusion trigger. [Evidence details](EVIDENCE_CLASSIFICATION.md#laboratory-examples).

**Public clinical sources:** [PBM](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12257489/).

<a id="ventilation"></a>
## Carbon dioxide retention and acidemia

**Paired case context — synthetic example:** The ARDS female example has increased somnolence or dyssynchrony; a postoperative male has shallow breathing after analgesia.

**Investigation and purpose — context-dependent clinical practice:** Arterial blood gas with documented oxygen/support conditions, interpreted alongside examination and delivered ventilation.

**Illustrative finding — synthetic data / clinical interpretation:** Fictional pH 7.28, PaCO2 60 mmHg and bicarbonate 27 mmol/L demonstrate acidemia with a respiratory component; baseline and other data are needed for acute/chronic or mixed classification.

**Effect on care — context-dependent clinical practice:** The team reviews airway patency, minute ventilation, mechanics and medication effects. Raising FiO2 alone does not correct inadequate ventilation. Sampling error or a venous sample changes interpretation.

**Classification note:** General clinical knowledge plus synthetic numerical interpretation; this is not a guideline-defined intubation or ventilator-adjustment threshold. [Evidence details](EVIDENCE_CLASSIFICATION.md#laboratory-examples).

**Public clinical sources:** [GAS](https://www.aarc.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/davis-et-al-2013-aarc-clinical-practice-guideline-blood-gas-analysis-and-hemoximetry-2013.pdf), [VENTASSESS](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11298231/).

<a id="oxygenation"></a>
## Oxygenation under defined support

**Paired case context — synthetic example:** A male pneumonia/ARDS example has persistent hypoxemia; the female lung-transplant example develops a new oxygen requirement.

**Investigation and purpose — context-dependent clinical practice:** An arterial gas when it can resolve the oxygenation question, with FiO2, PEEP/support and sample time recorded.

**Illustrative finding — synthetic data / clinical interpretation:** PaO2 80 mmHg on FiO2 0.50 gives a P:F ratio of 160 mmHg. This calculation alone does not diagnose ARDS or graft dysfunction.

**Effect on care — context-dependent clinical practice:** The team combines the finding with imaging, timing and competing cardiac/fluid/airway causes, then reassesses support and the underlying cause. A different support condition makes a serial ratio less comparable.

**Classification note:** General clinical knowledge/arithmetic; the P:F calculation is not a complete diagnostic criterion or treatment instruction. [Evidence details](EVIDENCE_CLASSIFICATION.md#laboratory-examples).

**Public clinical sources:** [GAS](https://www.aarc.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/davis-et-al-2013-aarc-clinical-practice-guideline-blood-gas-analysis-and-hemoximetry-2013.pdf), [ARDS](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10870893/).

<a id="lactate"></a>
## Lactate in sepsis or postoperative shock

**Paired case context — synthetic example:** The abdominal-sepsis male example has poor perfusion; the CABG female example has congestion and a rising lactate.

**Investigation and purpose — context-dependent clinical practice:** Lactate when shock/sepsis evaluation calls for it, alongside pressure, examination, urine output and cardiac/source assessment.

**Illustrative finding — synthetic data / clinical interpretation:** A fictional lactate trend from 4 to 3 mmol/L is one improving measurement, not proof that shock has resolved. Production, clearance and treatments affect the value.

**Effect on care — context-dependent clinical practice:** The team reassesses perfusion and the cause. Persistent elevation does not automatically mandate more fluid, particularly when congestion or respiratory deterioration is present.

**Classification note:** Guideline recommendation G24 is conditional (low certainty) for measuring lactate in sepsis; interpretation and response in this case are context-dependent. [Evidence details](EVIDENCE_CLASSIFICATION.md#laboratory-examples).

**Public clinical sources:** [SEPSIS](https://sccm.org/clinical-resources/guidelines/guidelines/surviving-sepsis-campaign-international-guidelines-for-management-of-sepsis-and-septic-shock-2026), [HEMO](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10175734/).

<a id="kidney"></a>
## Kidney injury after major surgery

**Paired case context — synthetic example:** The aortic-repair male example has oliguria; the female heart-transplant example has worsening kidney function and medication exposure.

**Investigation and purpose — context-dependent clinical practice:** Creatinine/BUN, electrolytes and relevant acid-base measures, urine-output/balance trends; urinalysis or imaging when a specific cause is being evaluated.

**Illustrative finding — synthetic data / clinical interpretation:** A fictional creatinine rise from 1.0 to 1.6 mg/dL within 48 hours is compatible with an AKI creatinine criterion after measurement/context checks. It does not identify the cause.

**Effect on care — context-dependent clinical practice:** Assessment distinguishes perfusion, congestion, obstruction and renal injury/exposure. Dialysis is considered from clinical needs and trajectory, not this creatinine value alone.

**Classification note:** Guideline diagnostic definition G25 is not graded. The fictional creatinine change fits a definition component after context checks; the cause and management remain clinical assessments. [Evidence details](EVIDENCE_CLASSIFICATION.md#laboratory-examples).

**Public clinical sources:** [AKI](https://kdigo.org/wp-content/uploads/2016/10/KDIGO-2012-AKI-Guideline-English.pdf), [RRT](https://kdigo.org/wp-content/uploads/2016/10/KDIGO-2012-AKI-Guideline-English.pdf).

<a id="electrolytes"></a>
## Potassium and rhythm/perfusion changes

**Paired case context — synthetic example:** The valve male example develops bradycardia; the limb-revascularization female example develops reduced urine output after reperfusion.

**Investigation and purpose — context-dependent clinical practice:** Potassium and renal/acid-base chemistry; magnesium where the rhythm or exposure question makes it relevant, with ECG/clinical assessment.

**Illustrative finding — synthetic data / clinical interpretation:** A fictional potassium result of 6.2 mmol/L raises an urgent hyperkalemia concern. Sample hemolysis can mislead; confirmation is coordinated without delaying necessary treatment of a clinically dangerous presentation.

**Effect on care — context-dependent clinical practice:** The clinical team assesses electrical effects, medications, kidney function and the need for correction or renal support. This example is not a universal treatment threshold or replacement dose.

**Classification note:** General clinical knowledge and context-dependent urgent assessment. The fictional potassium value is not asserted to be one universal treatment threshold. [Evidence details](EVIDENCE_CLASSIFICATION.md#laboratory-examples).

**Public clinical sources:** [RRT](https://kdigo.org/wp-content/uploads/2016/10/KDIGO-2012-AKI-Guideline-English.pdf), [HEMO](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10175734/).

<a id="cultures"></a>
## New fever and a suspected source

**Paired case context — synthetic example:** The esophagectomy female example has shock with altered drain findings; the male pneumonia example has compatible respiratory findings.

**Investigation and purpose — context-dependent clinical practice:** Blood cultures in suspected sepsis and source-directed microbiology; respiratory sampling or other studies only when clinically indicated.

**Illustrative finding — synthetic data / clinical interpretation:** A positive culture must be interpreted for pathogen, collection quality, contamination, source and prior therapy. A white count alone neither proves nor excludes infection.

**Effect on care — context-dependent clinical practice:** Results help refine antimicrobial/source management. Sampling should not harmfully delay time-critical treatment. The general new-fever guideline excludes severe immunocompromise; transplant investigation follows the transplant service plan.

**Classification note:** Guideline recommendation G23 is strong (low certainty) for blood cultures in the stated sepsis population. Other source-directed investigations remain context-dependent; general fever guidance excludes severe immunocompromise. [Evidence details](EVIDENCE_CLASSIFICATION.md#laboratory-examples).

**Public clinical sources:** [FEVER](https://www.idsociety.org/practice-guideline/new-fever-in-critically-ill-patients/), [SEPSIS](https://sccm.org/clinical-resources/guidelines/guidelines/surviving-sepsis-campaign-international-guidelines-for-management-of-sepsis-and-septic-shock-2026).

<a id="anticoagulation"></a>
## Bleeding/thrombosis balance with anticoagulation

**Paired case context — synthetic example:** The mechanical-valve male example and female MCS example receive different anticoagulant therapies.

**Investigation and purpose — context-dependent clinical practice:** The assay appropriate to the actual agent and indication, such as INR for vitamin-K antagonist effect, or a protocol-defined aPTT/anti-Xa assessment for heparin. CBC and clinical bleeding/device findings add context.

**Illustrative finding — synthetic data / clinical interpretation:** An INR result cannot be treated as the effect of every anticoagulant. Results depend on medication, assay, timing and clinical context.

**Effect on care — context-dependent clinical practice:** The team evaluates bleeding and thrombotic risk before adjusting therapy under the actual valve/device plan. No common target can be assigned to both examples.

**Classification note:** General clinical knowledge and drug/device-specific practice; there is no universal assay or target across these therapies. [Evidence details](EVIDENCE_CLASSIFICATION.md#laboratory-examples).

**Public clinical sources:** [PBM](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12257489/), [MCS](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12524590/).

<a id="hemolysis"></a>
## Anemia or dark urine during mechanical support

**Paired case context — synthetic example:** The LVAD female example has dark urine and anemia; the Impella male example has an unexpected hemoglobin fall without an established bleeding source.

**Investigation and purpose — context-dependent clinical practice:** CBC, renal chemistry and selected hemolysis measures, such as plasma-free hemoglobin, LDH, bilirubin or haptoglobin, interpreted with device/imaging findings.

**Illustrative finding — synthetic data / clinical interpretation:** A rising LDH alone does not establish pump thrombosis. Hemolysis, blood loss and other tissue injury must be distinguished.

**Effect on care — context-dependent clinical practice:** The support team evaluates position, loading, circuit/device problems and patient effects. No speed or anticoagulant change is selected from one result.

**Classification note:** General clinical knowledge and specialist practice; no isolated marker establishes pump thrombosis. [Evidence details](EVIDENCE_CLASSIFICATION.md#laboratory-examples).

**Public clinical sources:** [MCS](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12524590/), [LVAD](https://www.mdpi.com/2077-0383/11/9/2575).

<a id="fluidacid"></a>
## Fluid, electrolyte and acid-base needs during CRRT

**Paired case context — synthetic example:** A male with multiorgan failure remains fluid overloaded; a female with AKI has evolving acid-base/electrolyte abnormalities.

**Investigation and purpose — context-dependent clinical practice:** Chemistry and appropriate blood gas assessment, fluid balance and urine output; monitoring is tailored to modality, anticoagulant and clinical course.

**Illustrative finding — synthetic data / clinical interpretation:** Improved potassium or bicarbonate after treatment reflects part of the response, not kidney recovery by itself. Delivered therapy and ongoing losses matter.

**Effect on care — context-dependent clinical practice:** The renal/ICU team adjusts the prescription to patient needs and tolerance; fluid removal is balanced against perfusion. No fixed effluent, fluid-removal or laboratory interval is implied.

**Classification note:** Guideline statements G26 are not graded; CRRT preference in unstable patients (G27) is conditional, 2B. Monitoring and adjustment remain individualized. [Evidence details](EVIDENCE_CLASSIFICATION.md#laboratory-examples).

**Public clinical sources:** [RRT](https://kdigo.org/wp-content/uploads/2016/10/KDIGO-2012-AKI-Guideline-English.pdf).

<a id="paireddata"></a>
## A displayed number that conflicts with the patient

**Paired case context — synthetic example:** A male LVAD example has unreliable pulse-oximetry signal; a female ventilated example has an unexpected gas result.

**Investigation and purpose — context-dependent clinical practice:** Check measurement method, waveform/signal, specimen identity/site, timing and current support; use an appropriate independent assessment when necessary.

**Illustrative finding — synthetic data / clinical interpretation:** SpO2 estimates saturation; it does not measure PaCO2 or prove adequate ventilation. Low perfusion and other factors can impair accuracy.

**Effect on care — context-dependent clinical practice:** The team investigates the patient and the measurement together instead of escalating support solely from an unverified value. A blood gas addresses a specific unresolved question.

**Classification note:** General measurement knowledge and official device-safety information, applied to fictional observations. [Evidence details](EVIDENCE_CLASSIFICATION.md#laboratory-examples).

**Public clinical sources:** [SPO2](https://www.fda.gov/medical-devices/products-and-medical-procedures/pulse-oximeters), [GAS](https://www.aarc.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/davis-et-al-2013-aarc-clinical-practice-guideline-blood-gas-analysis-and-hemoximetry-2013.pdf), [LVAD](https://www.mdpi.com/2077-0383/11/9/2575).
