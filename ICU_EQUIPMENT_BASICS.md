# ICU and CTICU equipment basics with clinical examples

**Evidence labels:** [Guideline/consensus recommendation](EVIDENCE_CLASSIFICATION.md#recommendations) (strength and population stated), **general clinical knowledge**, **context-dependent clinical practice**, and **synthetic example**. A case or original diagram is not a standardized treatment protocol.

28 October 2026. Nineteen established equipment categories are explained below: function, purpose, setting, case use and limitations. These are original educational explanations based on public clinical or official device sources. They do not reproduce operating manuals, pump adjustments or rescue procedures. Device models, indications and instructions differ.

The examples relate to the [18 procedure pairs](CTICU_PROCEDURE_EXAMPLES.md). Listing a device explains its clinical role; it does not mean both patients should receive it or that evidence supports routine use in every case. Clinical selection remains tied to an indication and reassessment.

<a id="ventilator"></a>
## ICU ventilator

**What it does — general clinical knowledge:** Delivers positive-pressure breaths through a breathing circuit; monitors delivered flow, volume and pressure with alarms.

**Why and where used — context-dependent clinical practice:** ICUs and suitable transport settings; used when a patient needs assisted or mandatory ventilation.

**Worked case connection — synthetic example:** In the CABG male example, it supports breathing during early recovery; in the female ARDS example, delivered support is assessed against mechanics and gas exchange.

**What clinicians must distinguish:** It supports the patient while causes are treated. Mode, alarms, humidification, tube/circuit and measured response need review; it is not an autonomous diagnosis/treatment system.

**Classification note:** General equipment knowledge; patient-ventilator assessment recommendations G13–G14 have distinct strong/conditional grades. [Evidence details](EVIDENCE_CLASSIFICATION.md#equipment-basics).

**Public source:** [VENTASSESS](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11298231/).

<a id="workstation"></a>
## Anesthesia workstation

**What it does — general clinical knowledge:** Integrates medical-gas delivery, agent vaporization, a breathing system/ventilator and monitoring; circle systems include CO2 absorption and scavenging arrangements.

**Why and where used — context-dependent clinical practice:** Primarily operating/procedure environments under anesthesia care; a different system from a typical ICU ventilator.

**Worked case connection — synthetic example:** Male and female surgical examples can receive inhaled anesthetic delivery and ventilation during the operation before transition to a postoperative support plan.

**What clinicians must distinguish:** Functions and checks depend on the actual machine. A working ventilator component alone does not prove correct agent delivery or an intact entire breathing system. The cited older review explains foundations, not a current model manual.

**Classification note:** General equipment knowledge from a clinical review; not a single standardized workstation configuration or operating sequence. [Evidence details](EVIDENCE_CLASSIFICATION.md#equipment-basics).

**Public source:** [WORKSTATION](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3821261/).

<a id="hfnc"></a>
## HFNC system

**What it does — general clinical knowledge:** Combines a gas source/blender, heated humidification and a nasal interface to deliver high-flow oxygen.

**Why and where used — context-dependent clinical practice:** Monitored acute-care settings, including ICU; selected acute hypoxemic and post-extubation populations.

**Worked case connection — synthetic example:** A suitable hypoxemic male may use HFNC; the recovering female needs support chosen for her actual extubation risk.

**What clinicians must distinguish:** It is not an artificial airway or guaranteed ventilation. Monitor breathing effort, oxygenation, tolerance and trajectory.

**Classification note:** General equipment knowledge; the selected clinical indication G15 has conditional guideline support. [Evidence details](EVIDENCE_CLASSIFICATION.md#equipment-basics).

**Public source:** [HFNC](https://publications.ersnet.org/content/erj/59/4/2101574).

<a id="niv"></a>
## Noninvasive positive-pressure system

**What it does — general clinical knowledge:** Applies pressure through an interface; CPAP and bilevel assistance have different functions.

**Why and where used — context-dependent clinical practice:** Acute-care areas with appropriate monitoring and response capacity; selected cardiogenic edema, COPD-related acidosis and other defined populations.

**Worked case connection — synthetic example:** A suitable cardiac-edema male or COPD-related-acidosis female illustrates why the cause affects support selection.

**What clinicians must distinguish:** Leak, tolerance, airway protection, secretions and response matter. It is not an obligatory step before intubation.

**Classification note:** General equipment knowledge; G16/G17 are strong recommendations in specific disease populations, not universal NIV use. [Evidence details](EVIDENCE_CLASSIFICATION.md#equipment-basics).

**Public source:** [NIV](https://www.thoracic.org/statements/resources/cc/niv-guidelines.pdf).

<a id="airway"></a>
## Airway devices and visualization equipment

**What it does — general clinical knowledge:** An endotracheal tube provides access to the tracheal airway; laryngoscopy equipment helps trained clinicians visualize placement.

**Why and where used — context-dependent clinical practice:** OR, emergency and ICU airway care; used when airway protection or invasive ventilation is required.

**Worked case connection — synthetic example:** Both failing-respiratory examples need a patient-specific airway plan; a tracheal-reconstruction patient needs additional surgical coordination.

**What clinicians must distinguish:** Equipment availability does not establish a safe airway plan. Suction, oxygenation, circulation and rescue arrangements accompany skilled use.

**Classification note:** General equipment knowledge and context-dependent airway planning; specific RSI statements G09–G11 and confirmation guidance G12 are separate. [Evidence details](EVIDENCE_CLASSIFICATION.md#equipment-basics).

**Public source:** [RSI](https://www.sccm.org/clinical-resources/guidelines/guidelines/guidelines-rapid-sequence-intubation), [TUBE](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9804892/).

<a id="capnography"></a>
## Waveform capnograph

**What it does — general clinical knowledge:** Displays exhaled CO2 over time.

**Why and where used — context-dependent clinical practice:** Airway confirmation and continuing ventilation assessment in appropriate clinical settings.

**Worked case connection — synthetic example:** After intended intubation in either example, sustained exhaled CO2 helps exclude esophageal placement.

**What clinicians must distinguish:** A numerical value or chest movement alone is insufficient. Unexpected loss of the waveform requires immediate trained assessment of the patient, tube, circuit and physiology.

**Classification note:** Consensus guidance G12 supports airway-confirmation use; the source does not claim international standards. [Evidence details](EVIDENCE_CLASSIFICATION.md#equipment-basics).

**Public source:** [TUBE](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9804892/).

<a id="oximeter"></a>
## Pulse oximeter

**What it does — general clinical knowledge:** Uses a sensor to estimate arterial oxygen saturation and pulse rate.

**Why and where used — context-dependent clinical practice:** OR, recovery, ICU and other monitored care.

**Worked case connection — synthetic example:** Either respiratory example may have an apparently reassuring SpO2 despite a ventilation problem; low-perfusion device-supported patients may have poor signals.

**What clinicians must distinguish:** It does not measure PaCO2. Accuracy can be affected by perfusion, skin pigmentation and other factors; interpret the signal with clinical findings.

**Classification note:** General measurement knowledge and official FDA safety information; not a treatment protocol. [Evidence details](EVIDENCE_CLASSIFICATION.md#equipment-basics).

**Public source:** [SPO2](https://www.fda.gov/medical-devices/products-and-medical-procedures/pulse-oximeters).

<a id="arterial"></a>
## Arterial catheter/transducer

**What it does — general clinical knowledge:** Measures arterial pressure continuously and permits appropriate arterial sampling.

**Why and where used — context-dependent clinical practice:** Selected unstable ICU/CTICU patients and operations requiring close hemodynamic monitoring.

**Worked case connection — synthetic example:** In either shock example, waveform quality and clinical perfusion help interpret a pressure change.

**What clinicians must distinguish:** Leveling/zeroing and waveform quality matter; pressure is not cardiac output or proof of adequate tissue perfusion.

**Classification note:** General clinical/measurement knowledge; selection and interpretation depend on the clinical setting. [Evidence details](EVIDENCE_CLASSIFICATION.md#equipment-basics).

**Public source:** [HEMO](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10175734/).

<a id="central"></a>
## Central venous catheter

**What it does — general clinical knowledge:** Provides central venous access; a transduced catheter can measure central venous pressure and allow appropriate venous sampling.

**Why and where used — context-dependent clinical practice:** Selected ICU/CTICU patients needing central therapy or hemodynamic information.

**Worked case connection — synthetic example:** Congestion in a right-heart-failure example is assessed using venous findings with examination and echo.

**What clinicians must distinguish:** A CVC alone does not measure cardiac output; CVP is not a stand-alone fluid-responsiveness test.

**Classification note:** General clinical/measurement knowledge; selection and interpretation depend on the clinical setting. [Evidence details](EVIDENCE_CLASSIFICATION.md#equipment-basics).

**Public source:** [HEMO](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10175734/).

<a id="pac"></a>
## Pulmonary artery catheter

**What it does — general clinical knowledge:** Can provide right-heart/pulmonary pressure information and cardiac-output estimates by an appropriate method; mixed venous sampling may be available.

**Why and where used — context-dependent clinical practice:** Selected complex shock, right-heart or perioperative cases when information can change care.

**Worked case connection — synthetic example:** In a complex cardiac-failure pair, measurements complement echo and clinical perfusion to clarify physiology.

**What clinicians must distinguish:** It is invasive and not mandatory for every ICU patient. Measurement quality, technique and context affect interpretation.

**Classification note:** General clinical/measurement knowledge and selected specialist practice; not routine use for every ICU patient. [Evidence details](EVIDENCE_CLASSIFICATION.md#equipment-basics).

**Public source:** [HEMO](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10175734/).

<a id="infusion"></a>
## Infusion or syringe pump

**What it does — general clinical knowledge:** Delivers a prescribed fluid or medication at a programmed rate; some systems have drug-library safeguards.

**Why and where used — context-dependent clinical practice:** ICU/CTICU, OR and other clinical areas.

**Worked case connection — synthetic example:** Male or female patients may receive prescribed analgesic, sedative or vasoactive infusions through identified channels.

**What clinicians must distinguish:** A pump does not decide the correct prescription. Check concentration, programming, line routing, response and alarms; maintain a failure plan.

**Classification note:** General equipment knowledge and official FDA risk-reduction guidance; device safeguards do not establish a clinical regimen. [Evidence details](EVIDENCE_CLASSIFICATION.md#equipment-basics).

**Public source:** [PUMP](https://www.fda.gov/medical-devices/infusion-pumps/infusion-pump-risk-reduction-strategies-clinicians).

<a id="drain"></a>
## Pleural chest-drain system

**What it does — general clinical knowledge:** Allows pleural air/fluid drainage through a collection/water-seal or other prescribed system; suction depends on the system and clinical plan.

**Why and where used — context-dependent clinical practice:** Thoracic/pleural care and selected postoperative cases.

**Worked case connection — synthetic example:** The lung-resection pair illustrates why drain observations must be related to breathing and the operation.

**What clinicians must distinguish:** BTS guidance concerns pleural procedures. Cardiac mediastinal drains and post-pneumonectomy arrangements need the surgeon-specific plan; do not generalize suction/clamping rules across all drains.

**Classification note:** General equipment knowledge and procedure-specific practice; pleural guidance is not a standardized cardiac mediastinal-drain protocol. [Evidence details](EVIDENCE_CLASSIFICATION.md#equipment-basics).

**Public source:** [DRAIN](https://thorax.bmj.com/content/78/Suppl_3/s43).

<a id="pacing"></a>
## Temporary epicardial pacing system

**What it does — general clinical knowledge:** A pulse generator connects to surgically placed wires to provide indicated cardiac pacing.

**Why and where used — context-dependent clinical practice:** Post-cardiac-surgery care, including selected conduction or hemodynamic problems.

**Worked case connection — synthetic example:** A valve-surgery male with bradycardia illustrates an assessment that may involve temporary pacing; a female with a different rhythm may not need the same intervention.

**What clinicians must distinguish:** Electrical output is not proof of effective mechanical perfusion. Sensing, capture, rhythm and circulation require trained assessment; no settings are supplied here.

**Classification note:** General clinical/equipment knowledge and context-dependent specialist practice; the cited review explicitly rejects one correct pacing approach for every postoperative patient. [Evidence details](EVIDENCE_CLASSIFICATION.md#equipment-basics).

**Public source:** [PACE](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10433321/).

<a id="crrt"></a>
## CRRT machine and circuit

**What it does — general clinical knowledge:** Continuously provides extracorporeal kidney support using prescribed fluid/solute removal and replacement/dialysate arrangements.

**Why and where used — context-dependent clinical practice:** ICUs, including CTICU patients with AKI requiring kidney support; continuous therapy may suit hemodynamic instability.

**Worked case connection — synthetic example:** Male and female AKI examples have prescriptions matched to fluid, electrolyte, acid-base and perfusion needs.

**What clinicians must distinguish:** It does not repair the kidney itself. Access, anticoagulation, delivered therapy, fluid balance and electrolyte/acid-base effects need monitoring.

**Classification note:** General equipment knowledge; G27 is a conditional 2B modality recommendation for the stated unstable AKI population. [Evidence details](EVIDENCE_CLASSIFICATION.md#equipment-basics).

**Public source:** [RRT](https://kdigo.org/wp-content/uploads/2016/10/KDIGO-2012-AKI-Guideline-English.pdf).

<a id="vv"></a>
## VV-ECMO circuit

**What it does — general clinical knowledge:** Venous blood passes through a pump/membrane gas exchanger and returns to the venous circulation, supporting gas exchange.

**Why and where used — context-dependent clinical practice:** Specialist ECMO services for selected severe respiratory failure.

**Worked case connection — synthetic example:** The severe respiratory-failure pair may prompt specialist assessment for respiratory support beyond conventional care.

**What clinicians must distinguish:** VV support does not directly replace systemic cardiac pumping. Recirculation, gas exchange, bleeding and circuit/patient problems require expert assessment.

**Classification note:** General equipment knowledge and specialist selection; describing the circuit does not recommend it for every severe respiratory case. [Evidence details](EVIDENCE_CLASSIFICATION.md#equipment-basics).

**Public source:** [VV](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8315725/).

<a id="va"></a>
## VA-ECMO circuit

**What it does — general clinical knowledge:** Returns extracorporeally pumped/oxygenated blood to the arterial circulation, providing circulatory as well as gas-exchange support.

**Why and where used — context-dependent clinical practice:** Specialist cardiac/ECMO services for selected severe circulatory failure.

**Worked case connection — synthetic example:** The VA examples show why systemic perfusion, native heart loading and lung function still require assessment.

**What clinicians must distinguish:** Configuration affects physiology; limb perfusion, differential oxygenation and ventricular loading may be important. It is not interchangeable with VV-ECMO.

**Classification note:** General equipment knowledge and specialist selection; not a standardized response to any one pressure or lactate. [Evidence details](EVIDENCE_CLASSIFICATION.md#equipment-basics).

**Public source:** [MCS](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12524590/).

<a id="iabp"></a>
## Intra-aortic balloon pump

**What it does — general clinical knowledge:** A timed balloon inflates during diastole and deflates before systole to provide counterpulsation.

**Why and where used — context-dependent clinical practice:** Selected cardiac-support settings under a specialist team.

**Worked case connection — synthetic example:** A chosen low-output cardiac patient may receive it while the team treats the cause and evaluates response.

**What clinicians must distinguish:** It does not oxygenate blood or replace full cardiac output. Timing, vascular complications and the actual indication matter; this is not a routine-device recommendation for every shock patient.

**Classification note:** General equipment knowledge and selected specialist practice; no universal survival-benefit or routine-use claim is made. [Evidence details](EVIDENCE_CLASSIFICATION.md#equipment-basics).

**Public source:** [IABP](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11307388/).

<a id="lvad"></a>
## Durable LVAD

**What it does — general clinical knowledge:** An implanted pump moves blood from the left ventricle toward the aorta through an outflow graft; a controller, driveline and power supply support the system.

**Why and where used — context-dependent clinical practice:** Advanced heart-failure programs, postoperative CTICU and subsequent inpatient/community care.

**Worked case connection — synthetic example:** The paired LVAD examples link right-heart function, loading, alarms and perfusion.

**What clinicians must distinguish:** It supports systemic flow without oxygenating blood. Reduced pulsatility changes assessment; alarms require the LVAD team, not an automatic speed change.

**Classification note:** General equipment knowledge and specialist practice; a controller display is not a standardized management rule. [Evidence details](EVIDENCE_CLASSIFICATION.md#equipment-basics).

**Public source:** [LVAD](https://www.mdpi.com/2077-0383/11/9/2575).

<a id="microaxial"></a>
## Temporary left-sided microaxial pump

**What it does — general clinical knowledge:** A catheter pump across the aortic valve moves blood from the left ventricle into the ascending aorta.

**Why and where used — context-dependent clinical practice:** Selected catheterization-laboratory, cardiac ICU/CTICU and shock-team contexts.

**Worked case connection — synthetic example:** The Impella pair demonstrates access/limb and anemia/hemolysis questions during support.

**What clinicians must distinguish:** It is not an oxygenator. Position, filling, anticoagulation and access complications need specialist/device-specific assessment.

**Classification note:** General equipment knowledge and selected specialist practice; no universal device-selection or adjustment protocol is implied. [Evidence details](EVIDENCE_CLASSIFICATION.md#equipment-basics).

**Public source:** [MCS](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12524590/).

## Connecting the machine to the patient

A machine provides a defined measurement or form of support. The team checks whether it achieves the intended clinical goal, what unwanted effects arise and whether support can be reduced or needs reassessment. Equipment operation, clinical recommendation and a fictional case application are distinct. See the [public source register](CLINICAL_CARE_PUBLIC_SOURCES.md), [pain/airway/ventilation guide](AIRWAY_PAIN_AND_VENTILATION_EXAMPLES.md) and [laboratory examples](CLINICAL_LAB_EXAMPLES.md).
