# ICU and CTICU equipment basics with clinical examples

28 October 2026. Nineteen established equipment categories are explained below: function, purpose, setting, case use and limitations. These are original educational explanations based on public clinical or official device sources. They do not reproduce operating manuals, pump adjustments or rescue procedures. Device models, indications and instructions differ.

The examples relate to the [18 procedure pairs](CTICU_PROCEDURE_EXAMPLES.md). Listing a device explains its clinical role; it does not mean both patients should receive it or that evidence supports routine use in every case. Clinical selection remains tied to an indication and reassessment.

<a id="ventilator"></a>
## ICU ventilator

**What it does:** Delivers positive-pressure breaths through a breathing circuit; monitors delivered flow, volume and pressure with alarms.

**Why and where used:** ICUs and suitable transport settings; used when a patient needs assisted or mandatory ventilation.

**Worked case connection:** In the CABG male example, it supports breathing during early recovery; in the female ARDS example, delivered support is assessed against mechanics and gas exchange.

**What clinicians must distinguish:** It supports the patient while causes are treated. Mode, alarms, humidification, tube/circuit and measured response need review; it is not an autonomous diagnosis/treatment system.

**Public source:** [VENTASSESS](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11298231/).

<a id="workstation"></a>
## Anesthesia workstation

**What it does:** Integrates medical-gas delivery, agent vaporization, a breathing system/ventilator and monitoring; circle systems include CO2 absorption and scavenging arrangements.

**Why and where used:** Primarily operating/procedure environments under anesthesia care; a different system from a typical ICU ventilator.

**Worked case connection:** Male and female surgical examples can receive inhaled anesthetic delivery and ventilation during the operation before transition to a postoperative support plan.

**What clinicians must distinguish:** Functions and checks depend on the actual machine. A working ventilator component alone does not prove correct agent delivery or an intact entire breathing system. The cited older review explains foundations, not a current model manual.

**Public source:** [WORKSTATION](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3821261/).

<a id="hfnc"></a>
## HFNC system

**What it does:** Combines a gas source/blender, heated humidification and a nasal interface to deliver high-flow oxygen.

**Why and where used:** Monitored acute-care settings, including ICU; selected acute hypoxemic and post-extubation populations.

**Worked case connection:** A suitable hypoxemic male may use HFNC; the recovering female needs support chosen for her actual extubation risk.

**What clinicians must distinguish:** It is not an artificial airway or guaranteed ventilation. Monitor breathing effort, oxygenation, tolerance and trajectory.

**Public source:** [HFNC](https://publications.ersnet.org/content/erj/59/4/2101574).

<a id="niv"></a>
## Noninvasive positive-pressure system

**What it does:** Applies pressure through an interface; CPAP and bilevel assistance have different functions.

**Why and where used:** Acute-care areas with appropriate monitoring and response capacity; selected cardiogenic edema, COPD-related acidosis and other defined populations.

**Worked case connection:** A suitable cardiac-edema male or COPD-related-acidosis female illustrates why the cause affects support selection.

**What clinicians must distinguish:** Leak, tolerance, airway protection, secretions and response matter. It is not an obligatory step before intubation.

**Public source:** [NIV](https://www.thoracic.org/statements/resources/cc/niv-guidelines.pdf).

<a id="airway"></a>
## Airway devices and visualization equipment

**What it does:** An endotracheal tube provides access to the tracheal airway; laryngoscopy equipment helps trained clinicians visualize placement.

**Why and where used:** OR, emergency and ICU airway care; used when airway protection or invasive ventilation is required.

**Worked case connection:** Both failing-respiratory examples need a patient-specific airway plan; a tracheal-reconstruction patient needs additional surgical coordination.

**What clinicians must distinguish:** Equipment availability does not establish a safe airway plan. Suction, oxygenation, circulation and rescue arrangements accompany skilled use.

**Public source:** [RSI](https://www.sccm.org/clinical-resources/guidelines/guidelines/guidelines-rapid-sequence-intubation), [TUBE](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9804892/).

<a id="capnography"></a>
## Waveform capnograph

**What it does:** Displays exhaled CO2 over time.

**Why and where used:** Airway confirmation and continuing ventilation assessment in appropriate clinical settings.

**Worked case connection:** After intended intubation in either example, sustained exhaled CO2 helps exclude esophageal placement.

**What clinicians must distinguish:** A numerical value or chest movement alone is insufficient. Unexpected loss of the waveform requires immediate trained assessment of the patient, tube, circuit and physiology.

**Public source:** [TUBE](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9804892/).

<a id="oximeter"></a>
## Pulse oximeter

**What it does:** Uses a sensor to estimate arterial oxygen saturation and pulse rate.

**Why and where used:** OR, recovery, ICU and other monitored care.

**Worked case connection:** Either respiratory example may have an apparently reassuring SpO2 despite a ventilation problem; low-perfusion device-supported patients may have poor signals.

**What clinicians must distinguish:** It does not measure PaCO2. Accuracy can be affected by perfusion, skin pigmentation and other factors; interpret the signal with clinical findings.

**Public source:** [SPO2](https://www.fda.gov/medical-devices/products-and-medical-procedures/pulse-oximeters).

<a id="arterial"></a>
## Arterial catheter/transducer

**What it does:** Measures arterial pressure continuously and permits appropriate arterial sampling.

**Why and where used:** Selected unstable ICU/CTICU patients and operations requiring close hemodynamic monitoring.

**Worked case connection:** In either shock example, waveform quality and clinical perfusion help interpret a pressure change.

**What clinicians must distinguish:** Leveling/zeroing and waveform quality matter; pressure is not cardiac output or proof of adequate tissue perfusion.

**Public source:** [HEMO](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10175734/).

<a id="central"></a>
## Central venous catheter

**What it does:** Provides central venous access; a transduced catheter can measure central venous pressure and allow appropriate venous sampling.

**Why and where used:** Selected ICU/CTICU patients needing central therapy or hemodynamic information.

**Worked case connection:** Congestion in a right-heart-failure example is assessed using venous findings with examination and echo.

**What clinicians must distinguish:** A CVC alone does not measure cardiac output; CVP is not a stand-alone fluid-responsiveness test.

**Public source:** [HEMO](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10175734/).

<a id="pac"></a>
## Pulmonary artery catheter

**What it does:** Can provide right-heart/pulmonary pressure information and cardiac-output estimates by an appropriate method; mixed venous sampling may be available.

**Why and where used:** Selected complex shock, right-heart or perioperative cases when information can change care.

**Worked case connection:** In a complex cardiac-failure pair, measurements complement echo and clinical perfusion to clarify physiology.

**What clinicians must distinguish:** It is invasive and not mandatory for every ICU patient. Measurement quality, technique and context affect interpretation.

**Public source:** [HEMO](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10175734/).

<a id="infusion"></a>
## Infusion or syringe pump

**What it does:** Delivers a prescribed fluid or medication at a programmed rate; some systems have drug-library safeguards.

**Why and where used:** ICU/CTICU, OR and other clinical areas.

**Worked case connection:** Male or female patients may receive prescribed analgesic, sedative or vasoactive infusions through identified channels.

**What clinicians must distinguish:** A pump does not decide the correct prescription. Check concentration, programming, line routing, response and alarms; maintain a failure plan.

**Public source:** [PUMP](https://www.fda.gov/medical-devices/infusion-pumps/infusion-pump-risk-reduction-strategies-clinicians).

<a id="drain"></a>
## Pleural chest-drain system

**What it does:** Allows pleural air/fluid drainage through a collection/water-seal or other prescribed system; suction depends on the system and clinical plan.

**Why and where used:** Thoracic/pleural care and selected postoperative cases.

**Worked case connection:** The lung-resection pair illustrates why drain observations must be related to breathing and the operation.

**What clinicians must distinguish:** BTS guidance concerns pleural procedures. Cardiac mediastinal drains and post-pneumonectomy arrangements need the surgeon-specific plan; do not generalize suction/clamping rules across all drains.

**Public source:** [DRAIN](https://thorax.bmj.com/content/78/Suppl_3/s43).

<a id="pacing"></a>
## Temporary epicardial pacing system

**What it does:** A pulse generator connects to surgically placed wires to provide indicated cardiac pacing.

**Why and where used:** Post-cardiac-surgery care, including selected conduction or hemodynamic problems.

**Worked case connection:** A valve-surgery male with bradycardia illustrates an assessment that may involve temporary pacing; a female with a different rhythm may not need the same intervention.

**What clinicians must distinguish:** Electrical output is not proof of effective mechanical perfusion. Sensing, capture, rhythm and circulation require trained assessment; no settings are supplied here.

**Public source:** [PACE](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10433321/).

<a id="crrt"></a>
## CRRT machine and circuit

**What it does:** Continuously provides extracorporeal kidney support using prescribed fluid/solute removal and replacement/dialysate arrangements.

**Why and where used:** ICUs, including CTICU patients with AKI requiring kidney support; continuous therapy may suit hemodynamic instability.

**Worked case connection:** Male and female AKI examples have prescriptions matched to fluid, electrolyte, acid-base and perfusion needs.

**What clinicians must distinguish:** It does not repair the kidney itself. Access, anticoagulation, delivered therapy, fluid balance and electrolyte/acid-base effects need monitoring.

**Public source:** [RRT](https://kdigo.org/wp-content/uploads/2016/10/KDIGO-2012-AKI-Guideline-English.pdf).

<a id="vv"></a>
## VV-ECMO circuit

**What it does:** Venous blood passes through a pump/membrane gas exchanger and returns to the venous circulation, supporting gas exchange.

**Why and where used:** Specialist ECMO services for selected severe respiratory failure.

**Worked case connection:** The severe respiratory-failure pair may prompt specialist assessment for respiratory support beyond conventional care.

**What clinicians must distinguish:** VV support does not directly replace systemic cardiac pumping. Recirculation, gas exchange, bleeding and circuit/patient problems require expert assessment.

**Public source:** [VV](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8315725/).

<a id="va"></a>
## VA-ECMO circuit

**What it does:** Returns extracorporeally pumped/oxygenated blood to the arterial circulation, providing circulatory as well as gas-exchange support.

**Why and where used:** Specialist cardiac/ECMO services for selected severe circulatory failure.

**Worked case connection:** The VA examples show why systemic perfusion, native heart loading and lung function still require assessment.

**What clinicians must distinguish:** Configuration affects physiology; limb perfusion, differential oxygenation and ventricular loading may be important. It is not interchangeable with VV-ECMO.

**Public source:** [MCS](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12524590/).

<a id="iabp"></a>
## Intra-aortic balloon pump

**What it does:** A timed balloon inflates during diastole and deflates before systole to provide counterpulsation.

**Why and where used:** Selected cardiac-support settings under a specialist team.

**Worked case connection:** A chosen low-output cardiac patient may receive it while the team treats the cause and evaluates response.

**What clinicians must distinguish:** It does not oxygenate blood or replace full cardiac output. Timing, vascular complications and the actual indication matter; this is not a routine-device recommendation for every shock patient.

**Public source:** [IABP](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11307388/).

<a id="lvad"></a>
## Durable LVAD

**What it does:** An implanted pump moves blood from the left ventricle toward the aorta through an outflow graft; a controller, driveline and power supply support the system.

**Why and where used:** Advanced heart-failure programs, postoperative CTICU and subsequent inpatient/community care.

**Worked case connection:** The paired LVAD examples link right-heart function, loading, alarms and perfusion.

**What clinicians must distinguish:** It supports systemic flow without oxygenating blood. Reduced pulsatility changes assessment; alarms require the LVAD team, not an automatic speed change.

**Public source:** [LVAD](https://www.mdpi.com/2077-0383/11/9/2575).

<a id="microaxial"></a>
## Temporary left-sided microaxial pump

**What it does:** A catheter pump across the aortic valve moves blood from the left ventricle into the ascending aorta.

**Why and where used:** Selected catheterization-laboratory, cardiac ICU/CTICU and shock-team contexts.

**Worked case connection:** The Impella pair demonstrates access/limb and anemia/hemolysis questions during support.

**What clinicians must distinguish:** It is not an oxygenator. Position, filling, anticoagulation and access complications need specialist/device-specific assessment.

**Public source:** [MCS](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12524590/).

## Connecting the machine to the patient

A machine provides a defined measurement or form of support. The team checks whether it achieves the intended clinical goal, what unwanted effects arise and whether support can be reduced or needs reassessment. Equipment operation, clinical recommendation and a fictional case application are distinct. See the [public source register](CLINICAL_CARE_PUBLIC_SOURCES.md), [pain/airway/ventilation guide](AIRWAY_PAIN_AND_VENTILATION_EXAMPLES.md) and [laboratory examples](CLINICAL_LAB_EXAMPLES.md).
