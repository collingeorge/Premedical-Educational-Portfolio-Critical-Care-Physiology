# Simulated Case Set 02: Advanced Hemodynamic Scenarios
## 8-Scenario Comprehensive Analysis

Educational scenario set for physiology-based reasoning

## Scenario 1 — Post-CABG (POD0), Hypotension, Vasoplegia vs Pump Failure

**Status:** Hypotension MAP 58 on norepi 0.12 + vaso 0.03, CI 2.0, lactate 3.1 rising, temp 35.5°C, chest tube 120 mL/hr.

**Assessment:** Mixed vasoplegia vs early LV dysfunction. Hypothermia and mild ongoing bleeding contributing. Perfusion borderline.

**Reasoning and management considerations:**
- A full hemodynamic profile (CVP, PAOP, SVR, SvO2) would be essential for differentiating vasoplegia from pump failure
- If CI remains low, epinephrine initiation at 0.02–0.03 µg/kg/min would be a reasonable next step
- If filling pressures are low, a cautious 250 mL bolus could be considered
- TTE or Swan-guided assessment would be warranted if CI remains low despite inotrope and fluid optimization
- Rewarming to >36°C would be an important adjunct
- Chest tube surveillance with labs every 4–6 hours would be appropriate; re-exploration would be indicated if output exceeds 300 mL/hr or 200 mL/hr sustained over 2 hours
- Maintaining MAP at or above 65 would be a key hemodynamic target
- Early extubation could be considered if hemodynamics stabilize

**Educational discussion goals:** Stable MAP > 65, lactate downtrend, CI > 2.2, warming achieved, bleeding controlled.

**Considerations warranting urgent reassessment by the supervising clinical team:** Lactate continues rising despite interventions, chest tube output >200 mL/hr sustained, or MAP <60 on escalating pressors.

## Scenario 2 — VA-ECMO for Post-MI Cardiogenic Shock

**Status:** ECMO flow 3.8 L/min, lactate 5.2 (downtrending from 8), MAP 65, UOP low (10–15 mL/hr), CVP 14, limb ischemia risk.

**Assessment:** ECMO improving perfusion; AKI with venous congestion; possible limb ischemia.

**Reasoning and management considerations:**
- Hourly circuit monitoring (flows, pressures, hemolysis) would be essential
- Monitoring post-oxygenator SaO2 and pre-arterial-line ABG would help detect north–south syndrome early
- CRRT initiation would be a key management consideration for volume control and renal support (even balance initially, then gentle ultrafiltration)
- Avoiding additional fluids would be prudent in this setting
- Maintaining MAP at or above 65 with minimal catecholamines would be a reasonable target
- Hourly Doppler assessment of the left leg would be important; a distal perfusion catheter should be considered if perfusion worsens
- Lung-rest ventilation with FiO2 titration would be appropriate
- Daily echocardiography for LV unloading/pulsatility assessment would be warranted; monitoring for LV distension and north–south syndrome would be critical

**Educational discussion goals:** Stable MAP at or above 65, lactate improving, limb perfusion maintained, CRRT running.

**Considerations warranting urgent reassessment by the supervising clinical team:** Limb perfusion deteriorates, hemolysis develops, or LV distension on echo.

## Scenario 3 — HeartMate 3 LVAD (POD1), Borderline RV Function

**Status:** Speed 5100 / Flow 4.6 / PI 2.1, MAP 70 on NE 0.04, CVP 10, lactate 1.8.

**Assessment:** Early LVAD physiology; PI borderline; need to balance preload and avoid RV strain.

**Reasoning and management considerations:**
- Bedside echo assessment of septal position (neutral-to-left), RV function, and inflow alignment would be informative
- A MAP target of 70–80 would be appropriate, with gradual weaning of norepinephrine
- Preload targeting CVP 8–12 would be reasonable; avoiding CVP >12 would help protect RV function
- Continuation of low-dose heparin with transition to full anticoagulation once platelets recover would be the expected anticoagulation strategy
- Extubation could be considered if hemodynamics remain stable
- Management of ileus with NPO and NG, along with abdominal exams every 4 hours, would be appropriate

**Educational discussion goals:** Off pressors, extubated or ready, PI > 2.5–3, CVP 8–12, stable flows.

## Scenario 4 — ARDS from Pneumonia, Hypoxemia (PF approximately 87)

**Status:** PRVC, FiO2 70%, PEEP 12 (now increasing), Pplat 28, DP 16, MAP 68 on NE 0.06.

**Assessment:** Moderate–severe ARDS; meets criteria for proning; dyssynchrony risk.

**Reasoning and management considerations:**
- Increasing PEEP to 14–18 using the high-PEEP ARDSNet table would be a logical ventilator adjustment
- Prone positioning would be a high-priority intervention, targeting 16 or more hours, with cisatracurium infusion for 24–48 hours
- Deep sedation (propofol + dexmedetomidine) targeting RASS −3 to −4 would be appropriate
- Conservative fluid management with avoidance of boluses would be warranted
- One recruitment maneuver post-proning could be considered; additional recruitment maneuvers should be avoided if pressor needs are rising
- Trending ABG/lactate every 4 hours and maintaining PaO2 55–80, SpO2 88–95 would be appropriate monitoring targets
- ECMO triggers to consider: PF < 80 on FiO2 100%, DP > 20, Pplat > 30, pH < 7.20

**Educational discussion goals:** FiO2 < 60%, Pplat < 30, DP < 14, lactate trending down, stable prone cycle.

**Considerations warranting urgent reassessment by the supervising clinical team:** PF ratio falls below 80 despite maximal settings or driving pressure >20 persisting.

## Scenario 5 — POD2 Type A Dissection Repair, Hypertension + AKI

**Status:** SBP 152, HR 80, CVP 4, UOP 20–25 mL/hr, Cr 2.1.

**Assessment:** Dangerous postoperative hypertension; likely prerenal AKI; risk of renal malperfusion.

**Reasoning and management considerations:**
- A 250–500 mL crystalloid bolus targeting CVP 6–8 would be a reasonable initial step
- Continuation of esmolol to maintain HR < 80 would be appropriate
- Nicardipine initiation at 2.5–5 mg/hr, titrated gently to SBP 100–120 / MAP at or above 60, would be a key antihypertensive strategy
- Analgesia and dexmedetomidine for sympathetic control would be useful adjuncts
- Urinalysis and CTA abdomen (if oliguria persists after fluids) would be important diagnostic considerations
- If CTA confirms good renal perfusion and AKI persists, treating as ATN with supportive care and nephrology consultation would be the next step
- Avoidance of diuretics and nephrotoxins would be prudent
- Trending BMP every 6 hours would be appropriate monitoring

**Educational discussion goals:** SBP 100–120, UOP > 35/hr, Cr stable or down, CVP 6–10.

**Considerations warranting urgent reassessment by the supervising clinical team:** SBP >130 despite dual agents or sudden hypotension/rising chest tube output.

## Scenario 6 — Bilateral Lung Transplant (POD0–1), Reperfusion Injury / PGD Grade 2–3

**Status:** FiO2 60%, PEEP 8 with worsening compliance (DP 22), ABG 7.28/52/66, +2.5 L since OR.

**Assessment:** Early PGD; high DP; volume overload; vulnerable graft.

**Reasoning and management considerations:**
- Lowering PEEP to 6, then 5 if tolerated, while maintaining DP < 15 would be a reasonable ventilator strategy
- Avoidance of high PEEP and recruitment maneuvers would be important to protect bronchial anastomoses
- Tidal volume of 6 mL/kg would be standard; consideration of 4–5 mL/kg if DP remains high would be appropriate
- Strict permissive hypercapnia (pH at or above 7.25) would be acceptable
- Aggressive diuresis with furosemide 20–40 mg, escalating to a drip if needed, targeting −500 to −1,000 mL by morning would be an important volume management strategy
- Maintaining MAP at or above 65 with norepinephrine rather than fluids would be preferred
- Continuation of basiliximab, tacrolimus, and steroids would be the immunosuppression plan
- BAL in the morning could be considered if oxygenation allows
- ECMO would be a consideration if PF < 100 on FiO2 100%, DP > 20, or Pplat > 30 despite optimization

**Educational discussion goals:** FiO2 < 50%, DP < 15, pH at or above 7.25, net −0.5 to −1 L, CVP 6–8.

**Considerations warranting urgent reassessment by the supervising clinical team:** PF ratio <100 on maximal settings or DP >20 persisting despite adjustments.

## Scenario 7 — Septic Shock, Open Abdomen, Refractory Hypotension

**Status:** MAP 54 on NE 0.18, lactate 6.5 rising, CVP 14, UOP 5–10 mL/hr.

**Assessment:** Severe distributive shock + venous congestion; high risk of multi-organ failure; abdominal compartment physiology possible.

**Reasoning and management considerations:**
- Vasopressin addition at 0.03 U/min would be a reasonable escalation consideration
- If MAP remains below 65, epinephrine at 0.03–0.1 µg/kg/min would be a further escalation option
- Hydrocortisone 50 mg every 6 hours (after cortisol level obtained) would be an appropriate adjunct in refractory shock
- Further fluid administration should be avoided given capillary leak and positive 4 L fluid balance
- CRRT initiation would be a key management consideration (even balance initially, then ultrafiltration at 100–200 mL/hr once MAP is stable at or above 65)
- Bladder pressure monitoring every 6 hours would be important; loosening VAC if pressure exceeds 12 mmHg or physiology worsens would be warranted
- Continuation of piperacillin-tazobactam, vancomycin, and metronidazole would be appropriate; addition of micafungin could be considered if deterioration occurs without a clear source
- Trending lactate and ScvO2 every 2 hours would provide valuable perfusion data

**Educational discussion goals:** MAP at or above 65 on fewer agents, lactate improving, CRRT running, no ACS, no recurrent ischemia.

**Considerations warranting urgent reassessment by the supervising clinical team:** Lactate continues rising despite triple pressors, bladder pressure >15 mmHg, or hemodynamic collapse.

## Scenario 8 — SAH (Fisher 3, coil POD1) + Takotsubo Cardiomyopathy

**Status:** ICP 14, CPP 66, MAP 70 on low-dose NE; EF 30%; Na 150; risk of vasospasm (PBD2).

**Assessment:** High-risk neuro–cardiac conflict; CPP needs high MAP, cardiac function poor; nimodipine lowering BP.

**Reasoning and management considerations:**
- Maintaining MAP 75–85 (CPP at or above 65) would be the hemodynamic target; if clinical vasospasm is suspected, a transient MAP goal of 90–100 could be considered
- Continuation of norepinephrine would be appropriate; vasopressin addition could be considered if needed to minimize catecholamine burden
- Continuation of nimodipine every 4 hours would be important, with anticipation of post-dose MAP drop; up-titration of NE by 0.01–0.02 µg/kg/min as needed would be a reasonable response
- Euvolemia with 80–100 mL/hr maintenance would be the fluid strategy; a 250 mL bolus would be considered only if CPP falls below 60 after MAP has been optimized
- Maintaining Na 145–155 with 3% saline drip at 30 mL/hr and BMP every 6 hours would be the sodium management approach
- Frequent neurologic reassessment would be important, along with maintenance of normal capnia
- Daily TCDs would be appropriate for vasospasm surveillance; CTA would be indicated if the exam worsens
- Delaying extubation 24–48 hours until neuro-stable and cardiac-stable would be a prudent approach
- Troponin/BNP daily and repeat echo in 24–48 hours would provide cardiac monitoring

**Educational discussion goals:** CPP 65–70, ICP < 20, MAP 75–85, Na 145–155, stable neuro exam, stable EF, minimal pressors.

**Considerations warranting urgent reassessment by the supervising clinical team:** New neurologic deficit develops, ICP >20 sustained, or CPP <60 despite interventions.

## Cross-Scenario Educational Themes

**Hemodynamics:** 4 scenarios involving vasopressor support; 1 on VA-ECMO; 1 LVAD.

**Ventilation:** 3 scenarios with impaired compliance or ARDS physiology; proning and paralysis active.

**Renal:** 2 scenarios likely requiring or already on CRRT (ECMO + septic shock).

**Neuro:** 1 high-risk SAH requiring frequent neurologic reassessment and strict CPP control.

**Surgery:** 1 open abdomen with high ACS suspicion; 1 early lung transplant with PGD.

### Key Educational Discussion Points:
- **Scenario 7:** May deteriorate — re-exploration or ECMO consult would be considerations
- **Scenario 6:** Possible VV ECMO if oxygenation worsens
- **Scenario 8:** Vasospasm risk high; frequent neurologic reassessment would be important
- **Scenario 1:** Rising lactate, vasoplegia vs pump failure — close trending warranted
